# Vocabulary sources for ListoLista (Mexican Spanish): what exists, what it is licensed for, and a pipeline

Researched 2026-10-21 (Mexico City evening; some web timestamps are UTC 2026-10-22). Every number below was produced by a script that is listed in "How to reproduce" at the end; downloads are under `D:\_GitHub\listolista\data\cache\vocab\dl\`, derived tables under `...\vocab\out\` and `...\vocab\out2\`, the pipeline prototype under `...\vocab\proto\`. CompaPrecio was read only, never modified.

Companion report: `understand/aisles.md` (another agent, same day) covers the old matcher and the provenance of the old dictionary. Its conclusion (the 1,651 old terms were AI-generated in January 2026 with no source, `aisles.md` §4) is the reason this report looks for sourced vocabulary.

## Bottom line

- **Two genuine Mexican government sources cover most of a real shopping list, and both allow reuse with attribution.**
  - **PROFECO "Quién es Quién en los Precios" (QQP)**: 815 generic product names (329 groceries and household goods, 374 medicines, 112 durables), 46 categories, 673 grocery brand names, and package texts that hold everyday variety names (*Marías*, *concha*, *garrafón*, *molida*). Licence: CC BY 4.0, which I checked today in the datos.gob.mx record. The data runs from 2025-01-02 to 2026-08-31.
  - **INEGI ENIGH 2024, the household-spending catalogue (`gastos.csv`)**: 1,055 spending items in a COICOP 2018 tree; 343 of them are groceries, cleaning, health, pets and personal care. It uses Mexican words throughout (*Jitomate (tomate rojo)*, *Chile serrano*, *Queso Oaxaca o asadero*, *Hielo*, *Servitoalla*, *Jergas y trapos*). Licence: INEGI "Términos de Libre Uso" (attribution; do not imply endorsement). I found this source myself; it was not on the brief.
- **On my 60-word test, the best single source is ENIGH (37/60 with the right meaning), then QQP product names (36/60), Wikidata search (37/60, but no aisle and many wrong senses), Open Food Facts (26/60) and CLDR (15/60).** Taken together, at least one external source has 51 of the 60 words. QQP package texts bring that to about 55.
- **A prototype pipeline built from these sources alone produced 1,668 terms in 15 aisles: 20.3 KB raw, 9.2 KB gzip, 8.4 KB brotli.** That is well under the 15 KB budget. Every term traces back to a source row (`proto/out-no-overrides/sources.tsv`). It put 48 of the 60 test words in the expected aisle, 2 in a different aisle (both store-dependent) and left 10 unmatched. With 14 human override lines it reached 57/60. Those overrides were written after seeing the misses, so the 57 is not evidence of coverage.
- **What no source covers is the colloquial layer**: *papel de baño* (sources say *papel higiénico*), *cloro* (sources say *blanqueador*), *pasta de dientes* (QQP says *crema dental*), *garrafón*, *croquetas*, *bolillo* on its own, and *yogurt* spelt like that (QQP says *Yoghurt*). There is also almost no pet vocabulary: QQP has zero pet products. This layer must be a small, human-written override file per aisle, and every line needs a "why".
- **Emoji:** CLDR 49 (released 2026-10-21) gives Mexican names for the 132 food and drink emoji, for example 🍌 *plátano*, 🥜 *cacahuate*, 🥧 *pay*, 🩹 *curita*, and 🍅 with the keyword *jitomate*. An emoji whose CLDR name equals the word fits 10 of the 60 words, and a sensible emoji exists for about 24 (my judgment). CLDR keywords are loose (🫚 lists *cerveza*, 🧷 lists *pañal*, 🐮 lists *leche*), so emoji should come only from an exact name match or a human choice. Every aisle should always have its own emoji.
- **Not recommended as inputs:**
  - **Open Food Facts taxonomy**: its Spanish is from Spain (*patata* 27 vs *papa* 6; *zumo* 108 vs *jugo* 13; 0 *jitomate*, 0 *elote*, 0 *nopal*), corn tortillas have no Spanish name, and it covers food only. Licensing is unclear and either way copyleft: the file sits in an AGPL-3.0 repository, and the database it feeds is ODbL with share-alike.
  - **Retailer websites**: proprietary, and blocked or disallowed for bots.
  - **INEGI's INPC price-index basket**: 292 coarse items since 2024; ENIGH 2024 is finer and is the survey the basket is built from.
- **Wikidata (CC0)** is a good place to look up synonyms during review (*jitomate* is an alias of tomato, *durazno* of melocotón, *papas a la francesa* of papas fritas). It is not suitable as an automatic input: it has no aisles, and its top search hit is often the wrong thing (*crema* → an Italian town; *carne molida* → a footballer's nickname).

## 0. Method and the 60-word test

1. **Test list first.** I wrote 60 everyday Mexican shopping-list words, each with the aisle I expect, in `vocab/test-60.tsv`, before opening any external source.
   - **Disclosure:** I had already read the old checkpoint-1 dictionary (`old/checkpoint1/data/aisles-es-MX.txt`), so any comparison with it is biased in its favour.
   - The expected aisles are my own judgment, not taken from any source. Store-dependent cases are marked "varia" (store-dependent).
2. **Automatic matching** (`vocab/scripts/coverage.mjs`):
   - Text is folded: NFD, accents removed, lower case, punctuation turned into spaces.
   - Source labels are split into phrases: the whole label, text in parentheses, and alternatives joined by *o*, *y* or commas.
   - **E (exact):** the word, or a singular or plural form of it, equals a phrase.
   - **P (partial):** every word of the term appears, as whole words and in order, inside one phrase.
   - Whole-label matches win over split pieces, and CLDR names win over CLDR keywords.
3. **Meaning check.** I then marked automatic matches whose meaning is wrong (✗), for example *conchas* → the wine brand Concha y Toro, *tostadas* → Spanish toast. The "right sense" counts below are after this check. It is my judgment; the exact list is the `WRONG` constant in `scripts/table-60.mjs`.
4. **Prototype pipeline** (`vocab/proto/build-vocab-proto.mjs`), with a simple matcher (`proto/eval-60.mjs`): exact or plural match first, then the leftmost dictionary phrase found as whole words, longest first.

Aisle IDs used here (a proposal, 16 aisles): FRU Frutas y verduras · TOR Tortillería · PAN Panadería · CAR Carnes, pollo y pescado · SAL Salchichonería · LAC Lácteos y huevo · ABA Abarrotes · BOT Botanas y dulces · BEB Bebidas · CON Congelados · LIM Limpieza y hogar · HIG Higiene personal · FAR Farmacia · BBE Bebés · MAS Mascotas · OTR Otros.
- The checkpoint-1 plan had 14 aisles (`old/checkpoint1/docs/research/2026-09-25-aisle-sorting.md:191-193`).
- This proposal adds TOR and SAL because PROFECO's categories and ENIGH's codes keep tortillas and cold cuts apart, and Mexican stores have both counters.
- Whether to keep them separate is the owner's call.

## 1. PROFECO "Quién es Quién en los Precios" (QQP)

**What it is.** PROFECO's price survey: field staff record shelf prices. The open data is one RAR per year with half-month CSVs, 15 columns, and one row per product, store and date.
- **Local copy:** CompaPrecio already has the 2025 and 2026 archives downloaded and extracted under `D:\_GitHub\CompaPrecio\data\cache\qqp\extract\` (40 CSVs, 7.13 GB, 22,658,981 rows; `CompaPrecio/docs/research/profeco-qqp-2026-10.md:9-17`).
- **My extraction:** I streamed all 40 CSVs read-only with `vocab/scripts/qqp-vocab.mjs`: 416 s, 22,658,981 rows, the same total as CompaPrecio's survey.

**Licence (checked on the source's own record today).** `https://www.datos.gob.mx/api/3/action/package_show?id=programa_quien_es_quien_precios_2026` returned HTTP 200 with:
- `license_id: CC-BY-4.0`
- `license_title: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0`
- `license_url: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/`
- `metadata_modified: 2026-09-22T18:23:58`, organisation "Procuraduría Federal del Consumidor (PROFECO)" (saved as `vocab/dl/qqp-ckan-2026.json`).

Access notes and terms:
- The host answers 403 to a descriptive User-Agent. It answered 200 to Node's default `fetch` User-Agent.
- PROFECO's own pages carry no licence text (`profeco-qqp-2026-10.md:23-24, 615-616`).
- The Government of Mexico's "Términos de Libre Uso" (archived text quoted at `profeco-qqp-2026-10.md:622-654`) add three duties: cite the dataset name, agency, link and access date (AAAA-MM-DD); do not distort the data; do not imply official endorsement.

**Category scheme** (`vocab/out/qqp-categorias.tsv`):
- **46 categories** after removing accent variants (59 spellings) and **12 catalogues**: Básicos, Medicamentos, Frutas y Legumbres, Electrodomésticos, PACIC, Útiles Escolares, Mercados, Pescados y Mariscos, Juguetes, Especial, Navideños, Tenis (`profeco-qqp-2026-10.md:169-179`).

  - "Botanas y Bebidas" holds *Queso Crema* and *Dip*.
- **PACIC flag:** 31 generic products carry PROFECO's official anti-inflation basket flag (`profeco-qqp-2026-10.md:602-609`). That is a ready-made list of staples.

**Package texts (PRESENTACIÓN) hold the colloquial names** (`vocab/scripts/pres-mine.mjs`):
- *Galletas Populares*: **marias**, animalitos.
- *Pan Dulce*: **concha**, cuerno, bisquet, dona cubierta chocolate.
- *Pasta para Sopa*: **fideo**, codo, letras, munición, estrella.
- *Carne Res*: **molida**, bistec, diezmillo, falda para deshebrar, panza/menudo, carne para asar.
- *Chiles*: jalapeños en escabeche, rajas, chipotles adobados.
- *Agua Sin Gas*: **Garrafón 20 Lt. (sólo Líquido)** (in CompaPrecio's `presentations.tsv` with 8,828 rows).
- *Concentrado de Pollo*: caldo, cubos.

There is also noise in the same field ("diámetro", "no retornable", "mi villano favorito"), so these candidates need a human to tick them before use. Each kept one can then become a traceable term such as "galletas marias ← qqp:Galletas Populares/Marias".

**Strengths and weaknesses.**
- **Strengths:**
  - These are the names Mexican stores actually use, with Mexican brands (Maseca, Fabuloso, Zote, Suavitel, Valentina, Sabritas, Cloralex, Pinol).
  - National coverage; refreshed monthly.
  - A clean, permissive licence.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Survey-style labels: *Carne Pollo*, *Detergente P/ropa*, *Pan Blanco Bolillo*, *Yoghurt*.
  - Accents changed between months (`profeco-qqp-2026-10.md:139-150`).
  - Categories are not aisles.
  - No pet food; few baby items.
  - About 7% of base brands are store or house brands that span several aisles (Great Value, Aurrera, Precíssimo, Chedraui, Selecto Brand...).
  - Some brand names are ordinary words (*Fresca*, *Nido*, *Búfalo*, *Pronto*; *Corona* is 77% beer and 23% cold cuts).
- **Reachability:** `datos.profeco.gob.mx` returns 404 to GitHub's runners (`CompaPrecio/docs/PIPELINES.md:53-55`). The ListoLista build should therefore never fetch it. It should read a small derived catalogue committed to the repo; CC BY 4.0 allows redistribution with attribution.

**Coverage on the 60 words:**
- Product names: 37 automatic matches, **36 with the right meaning**. The wrong one is *sal* → *Picot. Sal de Uvas*, an antacid.
- Brands: 11 matches, 9 right. The wrong ones are *conchas* → Concha y Toro and *pollo* → the bouillon brand Riko Pollo.
- Package texts add *garrafón*, *concha* and *Marías*.

**The INPC basket ("canasta del INPC").**
- The 2024 update has **292 genéricos**, down from 299 (`dl/inegi/present_resul_2024.pdf`, extracted text lines 105 and 129: "Nueva 259 241 9 292"). It is built from ENIGH 2022 and CCIF 2018.

- The INPC open-data zip I downloaded (`conjunto_de_datos_inpc_indicador_mensual_csv.zip`, 34,802 bytes) holds only 7 aggregate indices, no generics.
- I did not find a file listing the current 292 generics: **unverified**.
- **Recommendation:** use ENIGH 2024 rather than the INPC list. ENIGH is finer (343 relevant rows against about 292 generics in total, including services) and is the survey the basket comes from.

## 10. Recommended build-time pipeline

Principle: **the phone gets one compact file; the repository keeps the evidence.** Nothing is fetched at deploy time; refreshing the sources is an occasional manual step. That means zero running cost, and the build does not depend on PROFECO or INEGI being reachable from GitHub.

**A. `tools/vocab/fetch-sources.mjs` (manual, a few times a year)** writes small, committed files to `data/vocab/sources/`. Each starts with a header: source URL, retrieved date, sha256, licence.
- `qqp-productos.tsv`, about 90 KB: PRODUCTO, CATEGORÍA, CATÁLOGO, rows, first and last date, top brands.
- `qqp-marcas.tsv`, about 70 KB: brand × category × rows.
- `qqp-variedades.tsv`: product × variety phrase from PRESENTACIÓN, with a `keep` column a human fills in.
- How to get them: read the PROFECO archives from CompaPrecio's cache or from its GitHub release `qqp-mirror` (`CompaPrecio/docs/PIPELINES.md:53-63`), and stream them as `vocab/scripts/qqp-vocab.mjs` does (about 3 to 7 minutes).
- `enigh2024-gastos.csv` (56 KB), fetched with HTTP Range from INEGI's zip (`vocab/scripts/zip-range.mjs`).
- `cldr49-emoji-es-MX.tsv` (about 30 KB), resolved es_MX → es_419 → es, with the emoji version from `emoji-test.txt` (`vocab/scripts/cldr-emoji.mjs`).
- `LICENSES/Unicode-3.0.txt`, required by the Unicode licence.

**B. Human files, in `data/vocab/`:**
- `aisles.tsv`: ID, Spanish name, English name, default emoji, default walking order.
- `map-qqp.tsv` (46 categories → aisle) and `map-qqp-products.tsv` (about 70 product exceptions, each with a `why`). Drafts are in `vocab/proto/`.
- `map-enigh.tsv`: about 70 COICOP prefixes → aisle; the longest prefix wins.
- **`overrides/<AISLE>.txt`, one file per aisle as asked**, with three kinds of line:
  - `+ término [= emoji]  # why`
  - `- término  # why` (drop a source term)
  - `término > LIM  # why` (move it to another aisle)
  - Start with about 50 to 100 lines covering the colloquial layer: *papel de baño*, *cloro*, *garrafón*, *croquetas*, *bolillo*, *pasta de dientes*, *yogurt*, *fideo*, *consomé*, *galletas marías*, pet terms.
- `synonyms.tsv`: *yogurt = yoghurt = yogur*; *papel de baño = papel higiénico*; *cloro → blanqueador*; *pasta de dientes = crema dental = pasta dental*.
- `brands-block.txt`: store brands such as Great Value, Aurrera, Precíssimo, Chedraui, Selecto Brand, Kirkland, Member's Mark. `brands-allow.txt`: common-word brands to keep, such as Fabuloso.
- `candidates/`: review queues that are **never shipped**: the old dictionary, Wikidata aliases, the QQP varieties not yet ticked. A line ships only after a person moves it into an override file.

**C. `tools/vocab/build.mjs` (every build; offline and deterministic).** Priority: override > QQP product exception > QQP category > ENIGH > derived head noun > brand. Emoji: override, else an exact CLDR name match within the allowed emoji version, else none (the app falls back to the aisle emoji).

Outputs:
- `src/vocab-es-MX.txt`, shipped, one line per aisle: `FRU:aguacate=🥑|cebolla=🧅|jitomate=🍅|…`.
- `data/vocab/build/sources.tsv` (every term → aisle → every source reference); `conflicts.tsv`; `review.tsv`; `dropped.tsv`; `report.json` (counts, sizes, test score).

**D. `tools/vocab/check.mjs` fails the build when:**
- a shipped term has no source reference;
- an override line has no `# why`;
- a term from `candidates/` appears without an override;
- a term is not folded, or appears in two aisles;
- the gzip size is over 15 KB.

It also prints the 60-word score and the checkpoint-1 `tests/aisles-es-MX.tsv` score. It warns when an override duplicates a source term in the same aisle (a redundant override).

**Expected size:** about 1,500 source terms after cleanup plus 100 to 300 overrides gives roughly 8 to 10 KB gzip, measured against the prototype's 9.2 KB for 1,668 terms. That is well under 15 KB.

## 11. Risks and open questions (owner's call where marked)

1. **Store-dependent aisles** (owner): *papel de baño* (HIG or LIM), *tostadas* and *tortillas* (TOR or PAN), *café* (ABA or BEB), *galletas* (ABA or BOT), *cajeta* and *miel* (ABA or BOT). The sources cannot decide these; per-list corrections, as checkpoint 1 planned, are the real answer.
2. **16 aisles or 14** (owner): keep TOR and SAL separate, as the sources and stores do, or merge them as checkpoint 1 did.
3. **Brand collisions:** *Corona* (beer, and a cold-cuts brand in QQP), *Fresca*, *Nido*, *Sol*, *Victoria*, *Roma*, *Pronto*, *Regio*. The matcher's "longest phrase, leftmost head noun" rule helps, and the review list catches single-word brands. *Roma* was not caught by my common-word check: **unverified** behaviour.
4. **Pet aisle:** essentially no source (QQP 0; ENIGH 3 generic rows; OFF 26 Spain-style lines). It has to come from human overrides.
5. **Emoji rendering:** check on the two test phones which emoji versions render; cap automatic emoji at that version.
6. **Freshness and reachability:** QQP data ends 2026-08-31 and PROFECO blocks GitHub's runners. That is why the derived catalogue should be committed and refreshed by hand. The vocabulary changes slowly, so twice a year is plenty.
7. **Legal reading:** CC BY 4.0 requires saying the material was changed. INEGI requires saying the transformation is ours. Both are in the text above. Neither agency's name may suggest endorsement. The about screen must not use their logos.
8. **My test's limits:** the 60 words and their aisles are mine, and the "right meaning" marks are my judgment. A list from real use by the two testers should replace it. The 57/60 with overrides is not evidence (the overrides were written after seeing the misses).
9. **Old dictionary:** 1,244 of its 1,651 terms have no source. Shipping them as they are would break the "genuine content only" rule. Treat them as review candidates.
