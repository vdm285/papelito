## Bottom line

1. **The old UX research is good and mostly real.** I re-fetched 27 of its URLs. 24 loaded and backed the claim. Meta's WhatsApp docs refused my fetch (HTTP 400), so that one is unverified. Two details are out of date or more nuanced than UXR says: the Android share in Mexico (now 72%), and haptics on iPhone. No URL was fabricated. Every fetched page existed with the cited title, including the 2026 NN/g, HN, arXiv, Notion and WebKit Safari 27 pages.
2. **The gap it names is real, and Mexico data makes it sharper.** People praise *simple* and *share with my partner*. They complain about ads, sync failures between spouses, account and verification nags, and losing Watch or widget support. In the Mexico store, Bring! (4,560 ratings) and Listonic (1,888) are the real competitors. AnyList (90 ratings, English and German only) and OurGroceries (160) barely exist there. Google Keep had 35 of its 100 most recent MX reviews at 3 stars or lower: crashes, sync, no iOS widget.
3. **Mexican users keep asking for one thing UXR never mentions: prices and a running total** ("te hace el total", "control del gasto", "agregar los precios"). It fits as an opt-in layer.
4. **Paper vs rows was never decided** (ROADMAP.md:84, AGENTS.md:77-78). The build shipped rows ("Renglones"). UXR's advice still holds: do not sync one big text blob. Give each line its own ID (UXR:21, 115-130).
5. **Undo instead of confirmation dialogs, a "Tachados" section, labelled Compartir plus one ⋯ menu, no zoom lock, never block the list on the network.** UXR backs all of these well (UXR:202-221), and checkpoint 1 built most of them.
6. **Voice means the keyboard microphone, not a custom button** (UXR:22, 151-154). I confirmed today that Spanish (Mexico) is on Apple's on-device dictation list and that iOS home-screen web apps don't get the Web Speech API.
7. **Missing from UXR:** glare, gloves and wet or busy hands; how WhatsApp opens links (an in-app browser has separate storage); keeping text you typed but didn't enter yet. Checkpoint 1 only adds an item on Enter, so a half-typed item is never saved or synced (`checkpoint1/src/app.js:593-596`). That breaks the Notepad benchmark.
8. **My view:** to beat Keep for two people in Mexico, don't add features. Make the "quantum moment" visible and dependable. It opens to *the* list instantly, from a WhatsApp link or an icon. What one person writes shows up on the other phone within a second. A strike there flashes here. Nothing is ever lost (undo, drafts, offline). The app remembers "lo de siempre". Details in §11.

## 3. Input models: "Papel" vs "Renglones"

**UXR's comparison (UXR:115-130):**
- **Raw paper (one textarea):** best for fast multi-item entry and the Notepad feel. Worst for tap-to-strike (taps place the cursor), surprise keyboards, accidental edits, concurrent edits and screen readers.
- **Paper-hybrid (Notes-style lines):** each line is an item with its own ID, struck with a margin circle (48 px column). Very good Notepad feel, merges per line.
- **Rows:** the input keeps focus after Enter, and the whole row is the strike target. The best store ergonomics and screen-reader story. Medium Notepad feel.
- **Why a whole-text blob is ruled out:** last-write-wins over a whole object silently erases one person's edit. Concurrent text needs OT or a sequence CRDT (Tyler Crosse 2026; Notion RGA, checked).
- **Verdict:** "test the paper-hybrid against rows. Do not ship a raw textarea with sync" (UXR:130).

**The two mock-ups (UXR:248-262):**
- **A "Papel":** a ruled sheet. Enter makes a new line and a new item. The margin ○ strikes, and struck lines stay in place.
- **B "Renglones":** an input pinned at the top that keeps focus. Rows at least 56 px tall, tap to strike, struck rows move to "Tachados", long-press for Editar/Borrar.
- **Test script:** 6 tasks on both phones; record taps and seconds to the first item, mis-taps and surprise keyboards.

**Status:**
- **Not decided.** "Papel or Renglones (try the mock-ups) ... Suggested default: Renglones" (ROADMAP.md:84). AGENTS.md:77-78 says Victor decides after trying them.
- **Mock-ups:** a claude.ai artifact (ROADMAP.md:33), not in the extracted folders. Unverified whether it still exists.
- **What got built:** Renglones, but with **two** input slots at the top (`checkpoint1/src/app.html:143-144`). That was carried over from the live app's "Input Waterfall" (`main/README.txt:19-23`: two fixed slots so the UI doesn't "jump" under your finger) and contradicts UXR's "one input" (UXR:250).
- **New items land at the bottom of the pending list** (sorted oldest first, `app.js:325-326`) while the input is at the top. So the code shows a toast "Añadido: … (abajo)" when the new row is off screen (`app.js:231-234`). That is a sign the layout fights the writing flow.

## 4. Strike, undo, clear

**What UXR recommends:**
- **Undo over confirm** (UXR:54, 206). Nielsen: confirmations for routine actions lose their power; "go to great lengths to provide undo" (checked).
- **Undo is cheap here.** The data model already keeps deleted items as tombstones (UXR:34).
- **Snackbar for about 8 s or until the next action** (UXR:181-183; Material's range is 4-10 s).
- **Strike = tap the whole row.** Remove the always-visible "×" next to the strike area (UXR:33, 212).
- **Struck items** go into a collapsible "Tachados (n)" with "Limpiar". Tapping one brings it back, as OurGroceries does (UXR:213).
- **Long-press** opens Editar/Borrar, matching the HIG meaning of touch-and-hold. Swipe is optional, and never from screen edges because of Android edge-Back (UXR:162-165, 212).
- **Microcopy** (UXR:241-242): "Se borró «leche» · Deshacer"; "Se limpiaron 5 tachados · Deshacer".
- **Open question** (UXR:294): should struck items stay in place (paper) or sink (rows)?

**What checkpoint 1 built:**
- **Undo** on delete, clear-struck and empty-list (`app.js:242-268`), shown for 8 s (`app.js:554`).
- **"Tachados (n)" with Limpiar** (`app.js:345-354`).
- **Long-press** at 550 ms (`app.js:374`), plus `contextmenu`, Shift+F10, and Backspace to delete with undo (`app.js:376, 382-386`).
- **The live app today** still uses `confirm()`/`alert()` (`main/index.html:411, 418, 466`) and 6 unlabelled emoji header buttons with the red wipe button among them (`main/index.html:196-201`).

**[opinion]** Keep all of this. Add one rule: **every** destructive or bulk action (Vaciar, Limpiar, delete, edit) is undoable, *including when the other phone did it*. A shared list's worst moment is "who deleted the milk?". A short "Ana borró «leche» · Deshacer" on the other phone would turn that into a non-event. The data model already supports it (tombstones with timestamps).

## 5. Sharing patterns

**What UXR says:**
- **Header** = title ▾ + a labelled **"Compartir"** with a status dot + ⋯ (UXR:210). Icons alone are ambiguous (NN/g icons, UXR:51).
- **Sharing opens the native share sheet directly** (`navigator.share`), falling back to copy plus "Enlace copiado". No confirm (UXR:206).
- **Share text:** "Nuestra lista del súper: <enlace>" (UXR:240).
- **WhatsApp preview** needs `og:title`, `og:description`, `og:url` and `og:image` (UXR:185-189; **unverified** today because Meta returned 400).
- **Home-screen trap on iPhone:** the installed app does not share storage with Safari, so the icon must be added **from the shared-list URL** (UXR:144; WebKit bug 181849). Show the install hint after sharing, on the shared URL (UXR:225).
- **The link is the key.** Whoever has it can read and edit. A "new link" button is planned for leaks (ROADMAP.md:97-98, 129).

**What checkpoint 1 built:**
- `navigator.share` with "Nuestra lista: <title>", falling back to clipboard and then `prompt()` (`app.js:534-543`).
- A first tap on Compartir silently turns a local list into a shared, encrypted one (`app.js:525-532`).
- An iPhone install hint after sharing (`app.js:543, 578-581`).
- Open Graph title and description but **no `og:image`** (`app.html:25-26`).
- Separate manifests for iOS and Android so the iPhone icon opens the list it was added from (`app.html:11-20`).

**Gaps [new / opinion]:**
- **WhatsApp's own browser (unverified).** I did not check whether WhatsApp opens links in its in-app browser on Android or iOS. If it does, the list the recipient sees there has separate storage from Chrome or Safari, the same trap as the iOS home screen. This must be on the phone test: tap the link *inside WhatsApp*, then later open the icon or the browser.
- **The recipient's first five seconds.** They tap a link from their partner and land on a list with items already in it. UXR's empty-state advice (UXR:55) covers the owner, not the recipient. **[opinion]** On first open of a shared link, show one line: "Esta lista es compartida. Lo que escribas aparece en el otro teléfono." Nothing else, and it goes away.
- **No `og:image` today.** Unless a sync-track or PWA doc covers it, the WhatsApp preview will have no picture.

## 6. Voice input

**What UXR says:**
- **Use the keyboard mic, not a custom button** (UXR:22, 229).
- **iPhone dictation** works anywhere you can type and keeps the keyboard open (checked). **Spanish (Mexico) is on-device** (checked).
- **The Web Speech API** sends audio to a server in Chrome, won't work offline, isn't Baseline (UXR:153), and isn't available in iOS home-screen web apps (checked).

**Not covered in UXR [new / unverified]:** Android, the 66-72% majority. Gboard's voice typing is the equivalent there. I did not check its es-MX support or whether it works offline. It needs a quick test on the Android phone.

**Splitting dictated text:** dictation often drops commas, so "huevos leche pan" arrives as one line (ROADMAP.md:87).
- **Current default:** only commas split; "huevos y leche" stays one item (ROADMAP.md:87, AGENTS.md:73).
- **The code:** splits on commas and then on " y " *inside the last comma part* (`app.js:211-218`). "huevos, leche y pan" becomes 3 items, "pan y mantequilla" stays 1.
- **[opinion]** Never split without commas automatically. Instead, when an added item has several words that each match the aisle dictionary, show a one-tap chip under it: "¿Separar en 3?". It is undoable, and it keeps "pan dulce" and "salsa de tomate" safe.

## 7. Phone-at-the-store ergonomics

**What UXR covers (sourced):**
- **One hand:** 49% of 1,333 people used one thumb (Hoober 2013, UXR:58). "Grips switch every few seconds" was not re-verified (UXR:281).
- **Reach:** the middle of the screen is easiest. Bottom sheets don't really improve reach (NN/g 2023). Edge targets take longer (NN/g Fitts), but making targets *touch* the edge helps (2026 preprint, checked). Hoober's sizes: about 11 mm at the top, 7 mm in the centre, 12 mm at the bottom.
- **Target size:** Apple 44x44 pt, Google 48x48 dp with 8 dp spacing, WCAG 2.2 AA 24x24 CSS px (UXR:59). Aim for 44-48 px with 8 px gaps.
- **The store view has one job: tap to strike** (UXR:57). Full-width rows mid-screen, at least 56 px tall (UXR:58, 250).
- **Edge gestures:** Android Back works from both edges, so no row swipes that start near an edge (UXR:162-165, checked).
- **Keyboard popping up in the store:** rows avoid it; raw paper triggers it often (UXR:122).
- **Offline in the store** is the biggest bug of the live app: a full-screen "Conectando…" overlay, and nothing is drawn if the sync library fails to load (UXR:14, 30-31, 205).
- **Keep screen on** (in the PWA research, not UXR): Screen Wake Lock as an opt-in menu toggle. iOS installed apps need 18.4+ (`docs/research/2026-09-25-pwa-platform.md:149, 289`).

**What UXR does not cover: glare, gloves, wet or busy hands.** I found no source for these in any of the four research docs (grep for glare, glove, sunlight). **[opinion, unverified]:**
- **Glare** (open-air tianguis, store entrances, car parks): keep body text at maximum contrast (`#111` on `#fff` is about 18.9:1, and checkpoint 1 already uses it, `app.html:29`). The struck and muted grey `#767676` is about 4.5:1, which is fine *because* struck items are secondary. Never put pending items in grey. Make struck state readable without color: line-through plus moved into "Tachados" plus a check mark. WCAG 1.4.6 (Contrast Enhanced, 7:1, page checked) is a good target for main text.
- **Gloves** are not a common Mexican supermarket case. Cold-room or winter-north use is an edge case. Capacitive gloves just need big targets, which the 56 px rows already give.
- **The real constraint is a busy hand:** cart, bags, a child, a phone held loosely. That argues for whole-row strike, no destructive control in thumb range, undo on everything, and **no accidental keyboard**. Tapping a row must never open the keyboard in the store.
- **Wet hands** (produce misting, unverified as common): stray touches are likely. Undo handles them.
- **Text size:** checkpoint 1 sets fixed `px` sizes (`app.html:77`: rows 17 px). **[opinion]** Use `rem` and let the system text size scale it. Whether Android Chrome's text-scaling setting affects `px` or `rem` differently is unverified.

## 8. Accessibility

**What UXR says:**
- **Items need checkbox semantics.** `<s>` and CSS line-through are never announced as struck (UXR:177-179, checked).
- **Real `<button>`s with Spanish `aria-label`s**, contrast at least 4.5:1, dark mode via `prefers-color-scheme` with no toggle (Apple HIG), and `prefers-reduced-motion` respected (UXR:214).
- **Never disable zoom.** iOS ignores `user-scalable=no` but Android Chrome, Firefox and Edge obey it, so the harm lands on the Android majority (UXR:156-160, 207). The live app still disables it, UXR:36.
- **Snackbar announced politely** (UXR:182).

**What checkpoint 1 built:**
- `role="checkbox"` and `aria-checked` on each item's text span (`app.js:362`).
- A sheet with `role="dialog"` and `aria-modal` that closes on Back (`app.js:392-417`), and a `role="switch"` for the aisle toggle (`app.js:425`).
- The snackbar as `role="status" aria-live="polite"` (`app.html:150`), dark mode (`app.html:34`), reduced motion (`app.html:83`), and a viewport without the zoom lock (`app.html:6`).

**Small gaps [new]:**
- `#pending` has `role="list"` but its children are not `role="listitem"` (`app.html:145`, `app.js:358-363`). The ARIA list pattern expects list items.
- The page is `lang="es"` (`app.html:2`). **[opinion]** `es-MX` may get a Mexican voice where the screen reader has one (unverified).
- **Remote changes are not announced.** If Ana strikes "leche" on her phone, a VoiceOver user on the other phone hears nothing. **[opinion]** Use one polite, batched live message, for example "Ana tachó 2 artículos", at most every few seconds.
- **Focus kept across re-renders** is a known leftover (ROADMAP.md:122). Every remote update re-renders the whole list (`app.js:318-356`), which can throw screen-reader focus.

## 11. My opinion: what would make it delightful, and better than Google Keep for two people in Mexico

**The bar to beat.** For an Android couple, Keep is free, already installed and offline-capable, and on Android it has a widget. Its weaknesses for *this* job:
- **It's a notes app** (inferred from MX reviews, not tested by me). You open it to a grid and find the note.
- **Sharing needs a Google account and an invite by email.**
- **Low reviews in Mexico** say it crashes, stalls on sync and has no iOS widget (§2.3).
- **It knows nothing about groceries:** no aisles, no history, no prices. That is my understanding; I did not check Keep's current features this session.

"No account" alone won't beat a pre-installed app. Being clearly better at the 30 seconds that matter will.

**What I would build, in priority order:**

1. **The first frame is the list.** It opens from the icon or from the WhatsApp link straight to *this* list: no grid, no spinner, no "Conectando". It is drawn from local storage before any network work (UXR:205).
   - Pass mark: warm open under 300 ms to readable items; cold open on 4G under 1.5 s (my targets, untested).
   - Here Keep's "find the note" step is the loss.

2. **Make the quantum moment visible.** When the other phone strikes or adds, the row flashes softly (checkpoint 1 has `.flash`, `app.html:81-82`) and carries a small mark of who did it: a colour or initial each phone picks once, no account.
   - Items added while you're in the store get a "nuevo" dot until seen. That covers "se me olvidó, ¿me traes…?" without push notifications.
   - Two people splitting the store ("nos dividimos en el súper", MX review) see each other's strikes within a second, so nobody grabs the same thing twice.

3. **Write like paper, shop like a checklist. One surface, not two modes.** **[opinion on the Papel/Renglones question]**
   - The list is rows: tap anywhere to strike, long-press to edit.
   - The composer is paper: a multi-line box that takes anything. Typed lines, keyboard dictation, and a pasted WhatsApp message ("Trae: huevos, leche, tortillas" or a "- " or "1." bulleted list) all become items, one per line or comma.
   - **Put the composer at the bottom, just above the keyboard, with the newest item right above it: the WhatsApp grammar** every Mexican phone user already knows. That ends the "(abajo)" toast problem (`app.js:231-234`). It also keeps the thumb near the keyboard while writing and the middle of the screen free for striking (UXR:58). (I did not check WhatsApp's dominance in Mexico this session. Treat it as an assumption to confirm.)
   - **Unsent text is never lost.** Save the composer as a draft. Commit an item after a short pause or on blur, not only on Enter. That is what Notepad would do (gap in §9).

4. **Undo everything, everywhere.** Any delete, clear or edit, local or from the other phone, is undoable for about 8 s, and "Vaciar" is one tap plus undo. The tombstone model makes this cheap (UXR:34). Nobody ever needs to ask "¿quién borró la leche?".

5. **"Lo de siempre."** People buy the same 30 things. When you type "le", offer "leche Lala 1 L" from this list's own past items (recognition over recall, NN/g, UXR:56). After a trip, offer the struck items as "¿Otra vez?" chips, one tap each. Keep has nothing like this, and it's the biggest time saver after sync.

6. **Mexican words, done right.**
   - **Matching:** accent-folded, quantities kept on display but stripped for matching ("1/2 kg de jitomate", "2 L leche", "garrafón").
   - **Aisle grouping:** optional and in *your* store's walking order, and later learned from the order you strike items (Grocery app precedent, UXR:91).
   - **Dictionary bugs:** fix the known ones first ("2 detergentes" → Otros, ROADMAP.md:58-70).
   - **Language:** Spanish (Mexico) by default and it *stays* Spanish (a Listonic complaint). English is a setting.

7. **Optional prices, because Mexican users ask for them.** Writing "tortillas 24" or "$24" adds a price. A small "Llevas $312" counts the struck items. Off by default, and it never changes how the item reads to the other phone. It lines up with the possible compa-precio merge (ROADMAP.md:26).

8. **Store mode with no mode switch:**
   - **Big rows:** 56 px or more, high-contrast text.
   - **Plain view while shopping:** with the keyboard down there are no controls in the list, only rows and "Tachados".
   - **Screen stays on:** Wake Lock offered once ("¿Mantener la pantalla encendida mientras compras?"), via `pwa-platform.md:289`.
   - **No surprise keyboard,** and no swipes from the edges.
   - **One short vibration on strike on Android.** On iPhone, maybe the single-tick switch trick, fragile and vendor-claimed.

9. **Trust that you can read in one line.**
   - **Header status:** "Al día" or "Guardado aquí · se enviará al volver". Never a blocking overlay.
   - **Under Compartir:** "Quien tenga el enlace puede ver y editar". Plus "Nuevo enlace" to cut off a leaked link.
   - **No ads, no nags, no rating prompts, no verification emails, ever.** These are exactly the complaints in §2.3.

**What I would not build:**
- **A custom mic button.** The keyboard has one.
- **An AI assistant.** The Listonic "worthless AI assistant" review; and it costs money.
- **A Bring!-style icon grid.** It slows paper-speed writing.
- **Categories on by default.**
- **Multiple stores at the start.**
- **Push notifications.** They need a server, which the zero-cost rule forbids. Some users dislike them anyway (Bring! MX 2026-08-15).
- **A Watch app or widget.** The web can't do it. Accept the gap and say so. On Android, the home-screen icon opening straight to the list is the substitute.

**How to know it worked (cheap, no accounts).** Re-run UXR's six-task script (UXR:252-260) on the real two phones, measuring:
- taps and seconds to the first item;
- mis-strikes and how often undo was used;
- surprise keyboards;
- the time between a strike on phone A and the flash on phone B;
- "did anything get lost?" after airplane mode on both phones.

Then 3-5 cold opens by people who were given only the WhatsApp link (UXR:262).

## Risks and caveats

- **The sync dependency conflicts with the new hard rule.** Checkpoint 1's "quantum" feel came from a Cloudflare Worker relay on Victor's account (ROADMAP.md:11-13, AGENTS.md:65-67). The new rule says nothing the owner must run. The main site uses the public HiveMQ broker in plain text (AGENTS.md:62-64). Whatever replaces them decides whether "under a second" is achievable. That's not UX scope, but every delight in §11 depends on it.
- **The Mexico review sample is small.** It's 100 reviews per app, iOS only (Android is the majority in Mexico; I did not pull Google Play), and the keyword counts are crude regexes.
- **WhatsApp:** the link-preview rules and how its in-app browser handles storage are both unverified.
- **Apple Groceries:** whether it now needs Apple Intelligence hardware is unverified.
- **Gboard:** es-MX voice typing offline on Android is unverified.
- **The old mock-ups** (claude.ai artifact) were not available to me.
